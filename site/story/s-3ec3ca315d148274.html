MIAMI (AP) — A Florida pastor with dual Haitian-American citizenship was convicted Wednesday of U.S. charges arising from the 2021 assassination of Haitian President Jovenel Moïse, a killing that plunged the Caribbean nation into deep turmoil. Christian Emmanuel Sanon was found guilty on all charges including conspiring to kill Moïse, capping a nearly eight-week trial in federal court in Miami. He had faced eight charges, the most serious being conspiracy to kidnap or kill a person outside the United States. Sanon, who remains in federal custody, showed little reaction as the jury verdict was announced. He faces up to life in prison and is set to be sentenced next Feb. 10. Prosecutors declined to comment after the verdict. But Sanon’s attorney, Zeljka Bozanic, said the defense team was disappointed and would file an appeal at the appropriate time. Haiti has been without a president since the assassination Haiti has not had a president since authorities said a squad of mostly retired Colombian soldiers killed Moïse and wounded his wife in a nighttime raid at their home on July 7, 2021. The poorest country in the Americas, Haiti has a history of dictatorship, political upheaval and foreign intervention that has long hindered democratic rule. Federal prosecutors said at trial that South Florida was a hub for planning and financing the plot to oust Moïse and install a president of the conspirators’ choosing. The prosecutors said Sanon was their initial choice for Moïse’s replacement. They also said Sanon helped raise money for the conspiracy and arranged travel to Haiti and lodging there for himself and others. Sanon had acknowledged in trial testimony that he was in communication at one point with people trying to replace Moïse, but said the group had moved on to another candidate long before the assassination. He denied any knowledge of plans to kill Moïse. Born in Haiti, Sanon moved to the U.S. as a teenager. He testified that he has worked with churches in Haitian immigrant communities in Florida and the Midwest. Prosecutors said South Florida was a hub for plotting against Haiti’s leader Prosecutors said the scheme to kill Moïse included paying about two dozen foreign mercenaries with money obtained from U.S. government programs that were intended to help businesses during the COVID-19 pandemic, according to court records. U.S. prosecutors have charged more than 40 people with crimes related to the assassination and a dozen have been convicted, including five who are serving life sentences. The plot to oust Moïse began taking final shape in April 2021, when Sanon met in South Florida with a former Haitian senator and several U.S. businesspeople, according to an indictment. The group agreed that Sanon, once in power, would award contracts to two Florida-based companies — Counter Terrorist Unit Federal Academy and Counter Terrorist Unit Security, collectively known as CTU — for infrastructure projects, security forces and military equipment, according to investigators. CTU initially hired about 18 Colombian nationals with military training to provide security for Sanon, according to prosecutors. Conspirators also spent months obtaining weapons and body armor and attempting to build ties with Haitian gangs, officials said. By June 2021, the conspirators realized Sanon had neither the constitutional qualifications nor sufficient popular support to become president. They shifted their backing to Wendelle Coq Thélot, a former Haitian Supreme Court judge who died in January 2025 while a fugitive. Sanon was once scheduled to be tried with four other men Sanon had been previously scheduled to be tried with four other men who were ultimately convicted last May of conspiracy in the assassination. But Sanon’s case was subsequently separated from theirs and delayed for health reasons.
